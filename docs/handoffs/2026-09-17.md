## 8. STRATEGY — two questions Karl asked at the close

**a) What are we missing about this situation?**

1. **The product is the harness, and the harness is now provably real — but it has only ever run one
   project.** Board, queue, briefs, cards, foreman, verifier, handoffs, rule 10, the Today page and
   the snapshot are all generic. The billing vocabulary, the bill feeder and the scanner are welded
   on. Nothing has tested whether a second project can be added as a folder rather than as a week of
   wiring. That test, not more lanes, is the launch.
2. **Initiative and identity live on different machines.** The box starts work by itself but each
   foreman run is an anonymous fresh process with no thread and no memory of its last attempt; the
   named agents have threads and memory but wait for a human to open a pane. Today's claim shows the
   box can pull; nothing yet lets it pull *as someone*.
3. **The thinking runs on the plan, not on the hardware.** Yesterday's eleven agents took the plan
   limit to 90 % in about six hours, while the box's GPU idled at 0 % except for 52 seconds. The box
   can watch, sample and rank; it cannot yet think well enough to build. "Near-zero cost on our own
   machine" is true of the eyes and false of the hands, and the schedule has to respect that until a
   bigger local model proves otherwise on a real task.
4. **Two of the loudest metrics cannot move without Karl.** O3 breadth needs a document only he may
   choose (Hop 0 is a control, not a gap), and the needs-you count is a KR graded on an agent that
   cannot close a card. Both are now named rather than quietly failing.
5. **The verifier-never-the-builder rule has paid for itself three times** in two days: a worker that
   restarted a unit against its brief, a "deploy every 10 minutes" that was a comment, and a tap
   proof that records a script as a human. Keep it even when it doubles the agent count.

**b) What should change about how we work?**

1. **One session, one number.** Yesterday's plan had seven lanes and produced good work and a 90 %
   plan limit. A session that names one number, with the lanes that serve it, will ship more and
   cost less. This handoff names one.
2. **Briefs should carry the budget.** Every brief already names its verify line; it should also name
   its ceiling (agents, wall-clock, and whether it may spend plan tokens at all). Nothing yesterday
   knew what it was allowed to consume.
3. **Hand work over as a goal plus a constraint, not as a lane list.** The best output of yesterday
   came from agents proposing inside their own domain; the weakest came from lanes assigned to
   whoever was idle. Say the number and the boundary; let the roster pick.
4. **Read the limit before dispatching.** The banner in any Claude pane is the only local reading of
   it, and it is now the first line of the next clock-in.
5. **Let the box own the cheap half permanently.** Sampling, ranking, snapshots, gate checks and
   report-only passes belong on the local model on the GPU; Claude should be spent on building and
   verifying. That split is what makes a daily cadence affordable.
